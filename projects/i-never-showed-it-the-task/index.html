I Never Showed It the Task

Composing human motion skills into unseen manipulation tasks with RL and a small VLA

Ayush Deshmukh

Robot learning challenge for Humanoid · work in progress, October 2026

Code (private until 8 Oct) Videos

TL;DR I filmed myself doing only four atomic skills with a phone: reach, push, pick-lift and place-down. A Franka Panda in Isaac Lab is then asked, in language, to do three combined tasks that were never demonstrated as a whole. The question: given exactly the same clips, does a VLA fine-tuned on them compose the skills by itself, or does it take RL over a motion vocabulary built from the clips, distilled back into a small VLA? Every model is scored on the same 100 fixed start states per task.

Speaker notesWho I am, what the challenge asked: use data I collected myself, drive a robot in sim, show VLA / world-model thinking. My angle: I never show the robot the task it is asked to do.

The tasks

The robot gets a language prompt and two camera views. My clips only ever show one skill at a time; none of them shows these combinations. Each task is scored on the same 100 start layouts, sampled once with a fixed seed inside the workspace the arm can actually reach (measured in simulation: about 0.3–0.78 m from the base with a vertical gripper).

C1 pick and place

pick up the red cube and place it on the green target needs reach → pick-lift → place-down. Success: the red cube rests within 3 cm of the target with the gripper open, for 0.5 s.

C2 stack

stack the red cube on the blue cube uses the same chain as C1, with 2.5 cm precision on top of the blue cube.

C3 push, then stack

push the blue cube onto the green target, then stack the red cube on it chains push → reach → pick-lift → place-down. It is the longest chain and the clearest test of whether skill hand-offs are learned. The scripted expert succeeds on 90/100 (shown at the top).

Speaker notesThree combined tasks, plus lift and push as atomic sanity checks. All are scored on the same 100 fixed layouts. Workspace came from measuring the arm's reach, not from guessing.

Two ways to compose

Both approaches start from exactly the same clips.

  • Standard (B1). Replay each clip in simulation with cameras on, label it with its atomic instruction (pick up the red cube), fine-tune SmolVLA on it, then prompt it with the combined task. Can a VLA chain skills it only saw one at a time?
  • Ours. Turn the clips into a motion vocabulary: each skill plus a small latent for direction, height and distance. Train RL whose action is pick a skill and its latent; the combined task appears only in the reward. Then distil the RL experts into SmolVLA, so one small VLA follows the combined prompts from images.

The hypotheses, each of which may turn out false. H1: B1 does not compose by itself. H2: given a planner, B1 still fails at the hand-offs between skills, not at planning. H3: RL in the vocabulary learns those hand-offs, so Ours beats B1 with a planner, most clearly on C3. H4: with the same RL budget, the vocabulary learns faster and moves more smoothly than raw actions, possibly at some cost in precision on C2.

Speaker notesThe novelty isn't a new algorithm. It's the controlled question: same clips, two routes. The combined task exists only in the reward.

The motion vocabulary

Each clip is resampled to 10 waypoints of gripper motion (position, yaw, open/close) relative to where the motion starts. A skill-conditioned VAE learns a 3-D latent per skill. For RL, one action is skill + latent, which decodes into a ~2 s motion tracked by the arm's controller. The RL agent chooses from human-shaped motions instead of tiny raw nudges.

Models compared

Every SmolVLA model sees only the front and wrist images, its own gripper state and the prompt. Only the RL experts and the scripted expert read exact cube positions; they are teachers and the upper reference.

Even lift needs two clips chained: reach, then pick up. B1 alone reaches the cube in 24 of 100 episodes and never grasps it. Told reach the red cube and then pick up the red cube, it lifts in 29. Nothing composes into C1–C3, and the failures sit at grasp and transport, the hand-offs between skills.

What didn't work

The policy that never lets go

The reward I started with only counted the cube as held while it was 2 cm up, so lowering it onto the target lost reward: PPO carried the cube over the goal and hovered. The next version paid grasp (gap < 65 mm) and success (gap > 65 mm) side by side, and the policy held the gripper at exactly 65 mm, flickering between the two. A third version only gave carry credit while grasping, and the vocabulary agent learned to lift the cube and hold it in the air forever. Each fix was checked by profiling the reward along the scripted expert, where it must rise at every phase.

A task the arm cannot do

C3 was first "the cube starts beyond grasp reach; push it closer, then pick it up". Measuring the arm showed this is impossible with a vertical gripper: to push a cube towards you, you must reach behind it, which is further away still. Tilting the hand gains only about 5 cm. C3 became "push blue onto the target, then stack red on it".

Speaker notesEach bug was found by watching the policy, not the return curve. The return looked fine every time.
